Zero Incidents Support

Days without a fight: a household safety sign for couples.

How it works

  • The safety sign — Home counts the days your household has gone without an incident, with your record and the last incident. The count changes at midnight.
  • Incident reports — tap File an incident report to log a quarrel: when, what it was about, a short statement and how bad it got. The sign goes back to 0.
  • Close the case — once you've made up, open the incident in History, tap Close the case and write down what you agreed.
  • The safety report — History › Stats shows the patterns, the charts and your safety awards.
  • Widgets — small and medium Home Screen widgets, and circular, rectangular and inline Lock Screen widgets, show the count without opening the app.
  • The Safety Officer — a calm coach that runs on your device with Apple Intelligence. It doesn't take sides and isn't therapy.
  • The Safety Toolkit — Cool down now (guided breathing and a 20-minute time-out), the make-up wheel, and short guides to calmer conversations.

Troubleshooting

  • The widget says "Open Zero Incidents" — the widget shows the count once the app has saved it. Open the app once (it starts counting on first launch); the widget updates within a few seconds. If it still says so, remove the widget and add it again.
  • The Coach tab says it's unavailable — the Safety Officer needs an iPhone or iPad that supports Apple Intelligence. Turn it on in Settings › Apple Intelligence & Siri, and wait for the model to finish downloading (it's faster on Wi-Fi and power), then tap Check again. On devices without Apple Intelligence, everything else in the app works, and the Toolkit has the same guidance as short guides.
  • The coach doesn't know about my incidents — in Settings, turn on Let the coach read my log. It reads a short summary, on your device.
  • The count looks a day off — days are counted by the calendar, so the number changes at midnight where you are. Before the first incident it counts from the date in Settings › Counting since, which you can change.
  • Deleting data — swipe left on an incident in History, or tap Delete incident on its report. To delete everything, delete the app: your log exists only on your device, so deleting the app deletes it for good. There is no backup on any server.

Tips

Zero Incidents is free, with no ads and no tracking, and every feature works without paying. If it's helped keep the peace at your place, you can leave an optional tip in Settings › Tip the safety committee: Coffee for the committee, Donuts for the break room, or Pizza party for the crew. Tips don't unlock anything; you get a thank-you, and that's all. Each tip is a one-off purchase through the App Store, so there's nothing to restore on a new device.

  • "Tips aren't available right now" — the App Store couldn't be reached (for example, you're offline). Everything else in the app works; open Settings again later.
  • "Waiting for approval" — the tip needs approval first (Ask to Buy, or your bank). The app says thanks once it goes through.
  • Refunds — Apple handles all payments, so refunds go through Apple: visit reportaproblem.apple.com, sign in with your Apple Account, and choose the tip.

If it's more than a squabble

Zero Incidents is for everyday disagreements. If there is abuse, violence or threats, if you're afraid of your partner, or if you're thinking about hurting yourself, this app isn't the right tool. People trained to help are there any time, for free and in confidence:

  • In immediate danger — call 911 (or your local emergency number).
  • National Domestic Violence Hotline — call 1-800-799-7233, text START to 88788, or chat at thehotline.org. 24/7.
  • 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline — call or text 988. 24/7.

The same numbers are on the Safety page at the bottom of the app's Toolkit tab.
